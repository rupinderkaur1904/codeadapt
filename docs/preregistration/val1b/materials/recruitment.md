# Recruitment criteria (draft)

**Nobody is approached until the owner approves the protocol and these materials, and the
institutional determination is recorded.**

## Authors (target 5, minimum 3)

**Include:**

- aged 18 or over;
- writes C++ comfortably (has written programs with loops, arrays and vectors);
- knows what binary search is.

**Exclude:**

- has read the CodeAdapt repository, design records, error models or evaluation reports;
- the project owner, or anyone who designed CodeAdapt content;
- anyone the owner grades, assesses or supervises, or who might feel unable to refuse
  (for example, students in a course where the owner is a teaching assistant);
- labellers in this study.

## Labellers (exactly 2)

**Include:**

- aged 18 or over;
- has taught, marked or reviewed introductory programming (for example as a teaching
  assistant, tutor or code reviewer);
- reads C++ fluently.

**Exclude:**

- the same groups as for authors;
- anyone who has seen CodeAdapt's error models or its outputs;
- the two labellers must not be in a position to coordinate their answers (for example,
  they should not label together).

## How to approach people

1. Use a personal message or an open call that states clearly:
   - that the study tests software, not the participant;
   - the time it takes;
   - [payment].
2. Send the information sheet first. Allow time to read it before asking for consent.
3. Make no follow-up pressure. One reminder at most.
4. Record only the participant's code, role and consent date in the study log, not in the
   repository.

## Why these exclusions

- Authors who know the error models would write bugs that fit them, which is the
  circularity VAL1-A and the critical review found.
- People in a dependent relationship with the owner may not feel free to refuse.
